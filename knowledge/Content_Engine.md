# Content Engine

**Use this file when:** you plan organic content, write short form video scripts, posts, carousels or captions, or turn one long piece of content into many.
**Covers:** pillars, giving versus asking, short form video, hooks, posts and carousels, captions, comment keywords, repurposing, recording rhythm, consistency, the language bank, learning from results, what to avoid, mistakes and a checklist.

## What organic content is for

Organic content has three jobs:

1. **Build trust.** Show, again and again, that the business understands the problem and knows how to fix it.
2. **Create demand.** Make the pain and the "Dream outcome" from `02_AUDIENCE.md` vivid enough that people want a fix now, not someday.
3. **Capture leads.** Turn interested viewers into people on the list, through the lead magnet or the main call to action.

Reach is only useful when it reaches people who could buy. Before writing any piece, name who it is for and what it should make them do next.

Decision rule: if a post could spread widely and still bring in nobody who fits "Dream customer", rewrite it or drop it.

## Content pillars

Pillars are the 3 to 4 recurring themes every post belongs to. Each connects a pain or goal from `02_AUDIENCE.md` to something the main offer solves.

- One pillar for each top pain in "Pains".
- One pillar for the main objection or wrong belief, from "What holds them back" and "What they have tried".
- One pillar that shows the work: behind the scenes, process, real examples, results from `05_PROOF.md`.

Example, a roofing company:

| Pillar | Pain or goal it speaks to | How it leads to the offer |
|---|---|---|
| Spot problems early | Not knowing a roof is failing until it leaks | The free inspection is the obvious next step |
| What a fair quote looks like | Fear of being overcharged | Their itemized quote, shown line by line |
| On the job | Not knowing what a crew will do at your house | Shows care and cleanup, lowers the fear of hiring |

Test: if a post in this pillar does very well, are the people watching potential buyers? If not, the pillar is too broad. Rotate pillars so no theme runs more than two posts in a row.

## Give much more than you ask

About four in five posts give: they teach, show or tell a story, and ask for nothing beyond a follow. About one in five asks: comment a keyword for the lead magnet, or take the main call to action. Never put two ask posts back to back.

The ask works because the viewer has already received something. In a plan, tag every post Value, Follow, Lead magnet or Main CTA, count the tags, and check the balance before handing over.

## Short form video

Short form video (Reels, TikTok, Shorts and similar) is the default organic format here, and these rules apply on every platform.

### The first 3 seconds

The opening decides whether the viewer stays. Three things happen at once:

- **On-screen text** of 8 words or fewer that makes sense with the sound off.
- **A spoken first line** that names a specific pain, result or surprise for this audience.
- **Something to look at** that is already happening: the problem itself, a hand pointing at it, the before shot.

Never open with a greeting, a name, "in this video", a logo animation or a slow intro. Start at the most interesting moment and explain after.

Example, an online fitness coach. On screen: "Why your lunch makes you tired". Spoken: "If you crash every afternoon, look at what's on this plate."

### On-screen text

Assume the sound is off and caption all speech. Keep text large, high contrast, few words per screen, and inside the safe area away from the platform's buttons.

### One idea per video

One video makes one point and stops. A second point is a second video.

### Beats

Build each script in 3 to 5 beats: the hook, why it matters (what the problem costs, in the audience's words), the point shown on screen, proof or an example, and one call to action.

Example, a bookkeeping firm, about 30 seconds:

| Beat | Spoken | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "This report shows which jobs lost money. Here's how to read it." | The report, one line circled |
| 2 | "Without it, you price the next job on the same wrong numbers." | A desk covered in invoices |
| 3 | "Look at this column first. What the job cost against what you billed." | Cursor moving down the column |
| 4 | "[ADD PROOF: a client example from 05_PROOF.md]" | A real report with names blurred |
| 5 | "Comment REPORT and I'll send you the template." | Text: Comment REPORT |

### Show instead of claim

Every adjective is a claim. Replace it with something the viewer can see. "We're thorough" becomes a walk through the checklist used on every job. "It's easy to use" becomes an unedited screen recording of the whole task (B2B software tool).

Formats that show: demos, screen recordings, real before and afters, behind the scenes, a real customer question answered on camera. If it cannot be shown or proven, cut it.

### Length

20 to 60 seconds. At about 2.5 spoken words per second, that is 50 to 150 spoken words. A single tip often fits in 20 to 30 seconds. A story can earn the full minute, but only when every second still moves the idea forward.

### End with one call to action

One ask per video. Value videos end with a follow line or nothing. Ask videos end with the comment keyword or the main call to action. Never stack "like, comment, share and follow", and skip the long outro.

## Hook patterns for content

More patterns live in `knowledge/Hooks_And_Headlines.md` if it exists. Every hook must be specific to this audience and offer. If a competitor could post it unchanged, rewrite it.

| Angle | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | Their words, then the cost | "I keep bidding jobs blind." Here's what that costs you. (bookkeeping firm) |
| Outcome | The result they want, made concrete | Know your profit per job before the month ends |
| Curiosity | A real detail that needs explaining | The first thing I check on every new patient (dentist) |
| Proof | A real result from `05_PROOF.md` | What changed when [client] stopped [habit] |
| Contrarian | A common belief, then why it is wrong | Skip the full workout on your busiest days (fitness coach) |
| Identity | Who this is for, by situation | If you run a crew and still do the books at night, watch this |
| Mistake | A mistake they are likely making | The quoting mistake that makes you look expensive (roofing company) |
| Story | The start of a real moment | The day a client brought us a shoebox of receipts |

Pay off the hook fast. If it raises a question, the video answers it. Write a main hook plus 2 alternatives with different angles, no more than two of the three sharing an angle. Story and Proof hooks must point at something real from the business files or the user.

## Posts and carousels

**Text posts.** The first line is the hook and must stand alone, because it is often all that shows before "see more". Short lines, one point, and an ending with the ask or a question that invites comments. LinkedIn posts up to about 200 words, Instagram up to about 120.

**Carousels.** Slide 1 is the hook, 8 words or fewer. Slides 2 to 8 carry one point each, readable at a glance. The last slide holds the ask. The cover promises something the swipe delivers: steps, mistakes, a checklist, a before and after, a myth and the fact. Example, a B2B software tool: "Five reports to check before Monday's sales meeting".

**Image posts.** A strong line from the language bank, a real testimonial from `05_PROOF.md` used with permission, or a photo from the work.

## Captions that add a new angle

The caption never repeats the video. It adds one thing: a detail the video had no time for, the story behind the point, an extra tip, an objection answered, or a question for the comments. Then it repeats the ask. Keep it to 2 to 6 short lines, with a first line that works on its own.

Example. Video: how to spot a lifted shingle from the ground. Caption: "We started making these after a customer watched a loose shingle for a whole winter before calling. Caught early, it's a small fix. Comment ROOF for our inspection checklist."

The story has to be real, from the business files or the user. Hashtags, if used, stay few and specific.

## Comment keyword calls to action

The comment keyword is the main lead ask in organic content: "Comment CHECKLIST and I'll send it to you."

- **One short word** in the deliverable language, easy to spell, tied to the lead magnet. Avoid words people type anyway, like "yes" or "great".
- **The same keyword everywhere**: spoken, on screen and in the caption.
- **Say what they get.** "The one page roof checklist" beats "more info".
- **Deliver the same day**, by hand or with a DM automation tool. The message links to the signup page, so the lead joins the list with consent and gets the welcome sequence from `/email`.
- **No free offer yet?** Ask posts point to the main call to action, worded exactly as in `04_OFFER.md`, and the user should run `/lead-magnet`.

Platforms have rules on automated messages and on engagement bait. Follow their current rules. Asking for a comment to deliver something real is not the same as asking for comments to inflate reach.

## Repurposing one long piece into many

One long piece (a webinar, a podcast episode, a long video, a recorded Q&A) holds many short pieces.

1. Get the transcript, with timestamps if possible.
2. List every idea that stands on its own: a story, a mistake, a how-to step, a strong opinion, a quotable line.
3. Keep the speaker's own words where they work. They sound more real than a rewrite.
4. Map ideas to formats. A typical yield: 5 short clips, 3 text posts and 1 carousel, with a caption per clip. A strong lesson can also become a nurture email.
5. Check that every clip makes sense alone, and add on-screen text that sets the context.

Repurpose winners too. Remake a post that did well with a new hook, or in a new format. Leave room in weeks 3 and 4 of a plan to remake the two best performers from weeks 1 and 2. An email that drew many replies also makes a strong post, and the reverse.

## A weekly recording rhythm

Batching turns content into one block of work a week.

- **Plan.** Pick the coming week's posts and finish the hooks and scripts.
- **Record.** One session for all of them, same setup, same light. Record each hook two or three ways so the edit has options.
- **Edit and write.** Cut the videos, add on-screen text, write the captions.
- **Schedule.** Load the whole week into the posting tool.
- **Review.** Look at last week's results before planning the next.

A phone, good light and clear audio are enough. Keep a running idea list from real sources: sales call questions, email replies, comments, objections the team hears.

## Consistency over volume

A plan the user can keep for 30 days beats an ambitious one they drop in week two. Posting days follow the capacity the user gave. Three posts every week builds more trust than daily posts for a week followed by silence. Keep a few finished posts in reserve, and when time runs short, post less often rather than worse.

## Using the language bank

The "Language bank" in `02_AUDIENCE.md` is the best source of hooks. Real customer phrases sound like the viewer's own thoughts.

- **Pain** quotes become Pain hooks or the first line of a caption.
- **Desired outcome** quotes become Outcome hooks or carousel covers.
- **Objection** quotes become videos that answer them.
- **Trigger moment** quotes become Story hooks.
- **Success** quotes become proof content only if the same quote is in `05_PROOF.md` with permission.

Example: "Won't switching make a bigger mess?" opens a video that then shows the switch, step by step. When comments bring a strong new phrase, offer to add it to the language bank, never without asking.

## Learning from your own results

Judge content by what it does for the business, not by views. A rough order of value:

1. **Leads:** keyword comments, signups, booked calls, messages that turn into conversations.
2. **Profile visits and follows** that came from a post.
3. **Saves and shares:** people kept it or sent it to someone with the same problem.
4. **Comments**, especially questions in the audience's own words.
5. **Views and likes:** useful for testing hooks, weak as a goal.

Where the platform shows where viewers drop off, find the beat that loses people and fix it first.

- Compare posts only with the account's own past posts, never with outside benchmarks.
- Each week, find the two posts that did best on leads, saves and shares, and note their pillar, format, hook angle and length.
- Remake winners with one change at a time, such as a new hook on the same body.
- Look for a pattern over several posts before dropping a pillar or format. One post is not a result.

## What to avoid

- **Trends with no point.** A trending sound or format is fine only if it carries a pillar idea for this audience.
- **Claims beyond the proof file.** No results, numbers, client stories or "most people" claims that are not in `05_PROOF.md`. Write `[ADD PROOF: ...]` instead.
- **Rage bait.** Starting fights, mocking customers or going after competitors draws the wrong people, and `06_RULES.md` forbids naming competitors.
- **Engagement bait.** "Comment YES if you agree" with nothing real behind it.
- **Fake proof.** Staged before and afters, stock footage passed off as the business's own work, actors posing as customers. Also never copy other creators' words or use audio you have no right to.
- **Loose handling of regulated topics.** Health, money, income and legal content follows `06_RULES.md` and local rules, in organic posts as much as in ads.

## Adapting to the language and market

- Write everything the audience reads in the language from `01_BUSINESS.md`, including the comment keyword. Template headings can stay in English.
- On-screen text limits are about reading speed. In languages with long words, use fewer words.
- Which platforms matter, and how blunt a hook can be, differs by market. Match the "Tone" and "Writing samples" in `03_VOICE.md`.
- Rules on disclosing paid partnerships, gifts and testimonials differ by country. Follow `06_RULES.md` and local rules.

## Common mistakes

- Opening with "Hi, I'm..." or a logo.
- Two or three points squeezed into one video.
- Captions that repeat the video word for word.
- Ask posts back to back, or every post ending in a pitch.
- Pillars that entertain but never connect to the offer.
- Daily posting for a week, then nothing.
- Judging a plan by views instead of leads, saves and shares.

## Checklist

- [ ] Every post belongs to a pillar tied to a pain from `02_AUDIENCE.md` and to the offer.
- [ ] About one in five posts asks, and no two ask posts are back to back.
- [ ] Every video opens with on-screen text of 8 words or fewer and a spoken hook that names a pain, result or surprise.
- [ ] One idea per piece, in 3 to 5 beats, 20 to 60 seconds read aloud.
- [ ] Each piece shows something instead of claiming it.
- [ ] A main hook plus 2 alternatives, each labeled with its angle, no more than two sharing an angle.
- [ ] Every caption adds a new angle and ends with the ask.
- [ ] The comment keyword is the same everywhere, and same day delivery is planned.
- [ ] Every result, number and story is in `05_PROOF.md` or the business files, or is a placeholder.
- [ ] The plan fits the posting capacity the user gave, in the language from `01_BUSINESS.md`.
