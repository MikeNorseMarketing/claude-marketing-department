# Ad Scripting

**Use this file when:** writing static ads or video ad scripts, choosing a video format, planning a creative test, or checking an ad against its landing page and platform policy.

**Covers:** static ad anatomy, video structure and timing, video formats, cold versus warm, proof without overclaiming, one idea per ad, page match, visual direction, alternative hooks, a small test plan, platform policy, adapting to the market, mistakes and a checklist.

## How this file fits

`Hooks_And_Headlines.md` covers the opener, this file covers the rest, and the `/ads` skill sets the format and character limits. All examples belong to made-up businesses with invented numbers. In real ads, results and credentials come only from `business/05_PROOF.md`, and price, guarantee and call to action only from `business/04_OFFER.md`.

## Static ad anatomy

| Part | Job | Guide |
|---|---|---|
| Image | Stop the scroll and show who it is for | One subject, readable at thumbnail size |
| Primary text, short | Hook plus a reason to click, before "See more" | Under 125 characters |
| Primary text, long | Build the case for the click | 80 to 200 words |
| Headline | The promise or offer, next to the button | Under 40 characters |
| Description | A small supporting fact | About 30 characters, often hidden |

**Image idea.** Write it as a direction someone can follow: what is in the frame, any text on it, and the format (1:1 or 4:5 for feeds, 9:16 for stories).
- Show the problem, the result or the person doing the work: a person mid-task, a bold statement on a plain background, a quote card with a real testimonial from `05_PROOF.md`, a three step diagram, a real product screen. Real photos beat stock.
- Text on the image is one short line, usually the hook or the offer.
- The image and the hook say the same thing, or the reader gets two ideas.

**Primary text, short.** Assume the reader sees only this. Hook first, then one line that names the offer or the reason to click. Example (dental practice): "Evening appointments for cleanings and checkups, Monday to Thursday. Book online, no phone call needed."

**Primary text, long.** For readers who tap "See more", in this order:
1. Hook, the same as the short version.
2. Problem, in their words, from "Pains" and the language bank in `02_AUDIENCE.md`.
3. What changes, from "Main offer" in `04_OFFER.md` and "What makes us different" in `01_BUSINESS.md`.
4. Proof, one item from `05_PROOF.md` with its context.
5. Call to action, worded exactly as "Main call to action" in `04_OFFER.md`.

Use short paragraphs. Problem agitate solve also works for pain led ads, if the agitation stays honest.

Example (bookkeeping firm, cold, problem aware):

> "I keep bidding jobs blind."
>
> That's how one contractor put it on a call with us. His books were three months behind, so every quote was a guess.
>
> We do monthly bookkeeping for construction companies. You get profit per job by the 10th, so you see which jobs make money while you can still change something.
>
> [ADD PROOF: one client result with a number]
>
> Book a free 20 minute books review at [link from 04_OFFER.md].

**Headline.** The last thing read before the click. State the promise or offer, not the brand name. Example: "Profit per job by the 10th".

**Description.** Often hidden on phones, so never the only place for a key point.

**Button.** Match the real next step, such as "Book now" for a call or "Sign up" for a free offer.

## Video ad structure

Five beats, in this order, scaled to the length.

1. **Hook (0 to 3 seconds).** Said, shown and written on screen at the same time.
2. **Explain the hook.** Pay off what it set up, or the hook feels like clickbait.
3. **Who is talking and why listen.** One or two lines, credentials only from `05_PROOF.md`. In a customer style ad, who they are and where they started.
4. **Proof.** One real result, testimonial or demonstration. Showing beats telling.
5. **Call to action.** One action, worded as in `04_OFFER.md`, said and shown. Say what happens after the click.

Plan for about 2 to 3 spoken words per second: roughly 35 words for 15 seconds, 75 for 30, 150 for 60.

| Beat | 15 seconds | 30 seconds | 60 seconds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0 to 3 s | 0 to 3 s | 0 to 3 s |
| Explain the hook | 3 to 8 s | 3 to 10 s | 3 to 15 s |
| Who and why listen | One phrase, or skip | 10 to 14 s | 15 to 22 s |
| Proof | 8 to 12 s | 14 to 24 s | 22 to 45 s |
| Call to action | 12 to 15 s | 24 to 30 s | 45 to 60 s |

Use 15 seconds for warm audiences and one proof point, 30 as the cold default, and 60 for a higher price or unfamiliar solution where explain and proof need room. Cut before you extend.

Example (online fitness coach, 30 seconds, Contrarian):

| Beat | Spoken | On screen |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | "I plan my clients' training around their calendar. Not the other way round." | Coach holding a phone with a weekly calendar. Text: "Training that fits your week" |
| Explain | "Every Sunday you send me your week. I send back three 25 minute sessions that fit, at home or at the gym." | The plan on screen, quick cuts of short home workouts |
| Who | "I'm [name]. I coach people whose jobs don't sit still." | Coach in a small home gym, name on screen |
| Proof | "[ADD PROOF: a client quote about training through a busy month]" | Client clip or quote card, with permission |
| Call to action | "Tap the button and book a free 15 minute call. We'll look at your week together." | Booking page on a phone. Text: "Book a free call" |

## Three video formats

**Founder on camera.** The owner or an expert talks straight to camera. Fits services, higher prices, B2B and coaching, where trust in the person decides the sale, and suits Contrarian and Story angles. Lead with the hook and introduce the person in beat 3.

**Customer style.** A person talks about the problem and result as someone who has been there, filmed casually. Fits consumer offers where a peer is trusted more than a company. Anyone who claims a result must be a real customer describing their own experience, with permission, and the result must be in `05_PROOF.md`. A paid creator can present the problem and demonstrate the product, but never claims results or poses as a customer. Disclose paid partnerships where required. Give speakers bullet points, not a word for word script.

**Screen demo.** The product doing the one thing that matters, with a voice over. Fits software, tools and courses with a visible process, for solution and product aware readers. One task start to finish, real screens only, private data blurred, never a feature tour.

Choose by what the buyer needs: to trust the person (founder), to see someone like them (customer style), or to see it work (demo).

## Cold versus warm audiences

Cold: people who have never heard of the business. Warm: followers, site visitors, the email list, past buyers.

| | Cold | Warm |
|---|---|---|
| Job of the ad | A reason to care | A reason to act now |
| Open with | The problem, a story or a surprising point | Proof, the offer or an answered objection |
| Call to action | Often a free offer from "Other offers" in `04_OFFER.md` | The main call to action |
| Proof | One clear, believable item | More of it, aimed at doubts |

Warm ads can answer the biggest objection from "What holds them back" in `02_AUDIENCE.md`, show the guarantee, tell one customer story, or walk through the "Customer journey" in `04_OFFER.md`. Never write anything like "We saw you visited our site".

## Using proof without overclaiming

- Proof comes only from `05_PROOF.md`, with the source noted per ad ("Proof used: 05_PROOF.md > Results > roofing client"). Missing proof becomes `[ADD PROOF: what is needed]`, never a likely sounding number.
- Keep the context. "One roofing client got current in 6 weeks" is a fact. "Get current in 6 weeks" is a promise. Never turn one case into "our clients get".
- Undershoot for skeptical or affluent readers. A typical result that is believed sells more than a best case that is doubted.
- Quote testimonials word for word, cutting only whole sentences. Without permission to use the name, run it without the name or not at all.
- Concrete details and demonstrations are proof too. The finished roof needs no number.
- Mention a guarantee only if it is in `04_OFFER.md`, never stronger.

## One ad, one idea, one call to action

Each ad carries one pain, one promise, one proof point and one action. When an ad says two things, the reader remembers neither.
- If describing the ad takes an "and", it is two ads, and a ready made test.
- Word the call to action the same way in the copy, on screen and on the page. Never "book a review" in one place and "get in touch" in another.
- Never offer a free guide and a call in the same ad. Choose by audience temperature.

## Matching the ad to the landing page

The click is a promise, and the first screen of the page must keep it. The page headline uses the same key words as the ad. The offer, price, terms and call to action are identical. The look is close, and an ad for one segment leads to a page for that segment.

Before handing over, compare the ad with the page or the file in `output/pages/`. If the page is missing or promises something else, flag it and suggest `/landing-page`. Never quietly bend the ad to fit a weak page.

## Visual direction for each beat

Write shots someone can film, not moods. "Engaging footage" is not a direction. "Close-up of a finger on the profit column of a printed report" is.

| Beat | What to show | Text on screen |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Movement in the first second, the problem or result in frame, a face close to camera | The hook in a few words |
| Explain | The situation, the process in action, or a simple graphic | One key phrase, or none |
| Who | The person in their real work setting | Name and role, credentials only from `05_PROOF.md` |
| Proof | The real report, finished work, testimonial clip or screen | Source, or the name with permission |
| Call to action | The page or booking screen on a phone | The exact call to action |

- Shoot 9:16 for stories and short video, 4:5 or 1:1 for feeds, and write shots anyone can film with a phone.
- Caption the whole video and assume the sound is off. Keep text and faces away from the edges where the platform puts buttons.
- Change the shot, framing or text every few seconds.

## Writing three alternative hooks per script

Every script gets 3 alternative hooks on different angles, with the body unchanged, so a test swaps only the first 3 seconds.
- Each must lead naturally into the same explain beat. Read it out loud with beat 2, and adjust the hook if the join feels off.
- Write each in all three channels, on angles that differ from the main hook and each other.

Example (roofing company, body explains how the inspection finds where water gets in):
1. **(Pain)** Said: "Third repair. Same leak." Shown: water dripping into a bucket in an attic. Text: "Same leak. Third repair."
2. **(Curiosity)** Said: "The stain is here. Let's find where the water gets in." Shown: the camera moves from a ceiling stain up toward the roof. Text: "Where does the water get in?"
3. **(Proof)** Said: "[ADD PROOF: number of roofs inspected] roofs in, and this is what we check first." Shown: an inspector with a moisture meter. Text: "What we check first"

## Building a small test

Test from the biggest lever to the smallest, one variable per round.
1. **Angles.** Three ads on three angles, for example Pain, Proof and Contrarian. Same format, audience, budget and page. Keep the execution cheap: statics or plain talking head videos.
2. **Hooks.** 3 to 5 hooks on the winning angle, everything else unchanged.
3. **Formats.** The winning angle and hook as founder, customer style, demo and static.
4. **Details.** Body length, proof item, call to action wording, the page.

Before launch, fix the deciding metric (usually cost per lead or per booked call, since a cheap click that never books has not won) and a stopping point, such as a set budget or number of days. Give every variant the same audience, budget and period. Log what won in the ads output file and start the next round from the winner.

## Platform policy basics

Rules change, and `06_RULES.md` and local law win over this list. When in doubt, tone it down and tell the user to check current policy.

- **Personal attributes.** Never assert or imply the viewer's health, body, weight, finances, age, ethnicity, religion, sexuality, gender identity, disability or criminal record, in copy, image text or spoken lines. "Are you in debt?" fails. "A plan for paying off debt" passes.
- **Before and after.** No side by side photos of bodies or appearance, and nothing implying a guaranteed change in how someone looks or feels. For things (a roof, a garden, a set of books) it can be fine if real, unedited and not implying everyone gets the same result.
- **Health.** No cures, no treating or preventing disease, no fast body change claims, no shaming. Say what the service does, not what it will do to the viewer's body.
- **Money and income.** No guaranteed earnings, no "get rich" or "quit your job" promises. Income, investing and credit offers carry extra rules. An income result must be real, from `05_PROOF.md`, in context, with any disclaimer the market requires.
- **Fake urgency.** Only real deadlines and limits. No resetting countdowns.
- **Fake interface and shock.** No fake play buttons, notifications or chat screenshots, and no shocking images.

## Adapting to the market

- Write scripts natively in the language set in `01_BUSINESS.md`. Translated spoken copy is heard at once.
- Directness, humor and how hard a call to action can push vary by market. Follow `03_VOICE.md` and undershoot when unsure.
- Use local faces, settings and currency formats, and only the testimonial details the permission notes in `05_PROOF.md` allow.

## Common mistakes

- A logo, an intro or "Hi, my name is" in the first 3 seconds.
- An explain beat that does not pay off the hook.
- The key point in the description, or short primary text that needs "See more".
- Two ideas or two calls to action in one ad.
- Proof that is not in `05_PROOF.md`, or one case turned into "our clients get".
- A cold ad asking for a big commitment, or a warm ad reintroducing the business.
- An actor claiming results in a customer style ad.
- Testing five things at once.

## Checklist

- [ ] One idea, one proof point and one call to action, worded exactly as in `04_OFFER.md`.
- [ ] The hook lands in the first line or 3 seconds, the spoken line, visual and text match, and the explain beat pays it off.
- [ ] Every claim comes from `05_PROOF.md` with its source noted, or is marked `[ADD PROOF: ...]`, undershot for skeptical readers.
- [ ] Fits cold or warm and the awareness level in `02_AUDIENCE.md`.
- [ ] Static: short primary text works alone, headline under 40 characters, image idea concrete.
- [ ] Video: word count fits the length, visual direction for every beat, captions, 3 alternative hooks on different angles.
- [ ] Ad and page match: same words, offer and call to action.
- [ ] No personal attributes, body before and after, health or income promises, fake urgency or fake interface.
- [ ] Written natively in the business's language and the voice of `03_VOICE.md`.
